About This Page

This page reflects the actual work AMS departments completed during our 10/3 session on Building an Intervention Menu. Each team inventoried their current practices, selected must-have interventions for each tier, and flagged the support they still need. What's here is what AMS teachers said, not a generic list.

How to read each card: "Must-Haves" are the interventions the team committed to. "Support Needed" captures open questions from Step 4 of the planning document. "Ideas to Explore" shows strategies the team wants to investigate further.

🧪 Science

Position on Must-Haves

"We use all of the above interventions. Paring it down diminishes the effectiveness of meeting all students' needs."

Science chose not to narrow their list — all inventoried practices remain in active use.

Support Needed

Training: Teachers not yet trained on Magic School AI Text Leveler need training on the resource room (text leveler, translation, summarizer).

Modeling: Sid Carden has completed training.

Logistics: Training for teachers and students on how to use the resource room; students need a few class periods of practice time to become familiar with the tools.

Ideas to Explore

Check-In / Check-Out (CICO) — Tier 2, K–12. Provides students with routine feedback on behavior and increased positive adult attention daily.

Reading Levels — Tier 1. Teach students to adjust text complexity themselves using Magic School AI's text leveler.

Distinctive Science practices

  • Amplify read-aloud options & Amplify Spanish version
  • Multiple reading levels for same content
  • Translated documents & 1:1 para support (Tier 3)
  • Differentiated worksheets (fill-in-the-blanks, reduced work)
  • Posting objectives and concepts; building background knowledge

🔍 Common Themes Across Departments

Patterns that appeared in nearly every team's inventory:

  • Organizational strategies (binders, notebooks)
  • Similar extensive Tier 1 / 2 / 3 "tool kit"
  • Speech-to-text and text-to-speech
  • Focus on building positive relationships
  • Graphic organizers
  • Different forms of checking in
  • Skeletal notes; exit tickets; sentence stems
  • Small-group discussions and share-outs
  • Modeling a new skill or learning target
  • Office hours; chunking work; student choice
  • Tier 3: modified curriculum and grading
  • Tier 1: clear routines
  • Tier 2: targeted small groups

🧩 Identified Gaps

Some teams are doing these; others aren't. These are opportunities to consider for school-wide adoption.

Common Writing Grading

Common grading across departments for writing — consistent expectations so students see the same standards in every class.

Student Self-Adjustment Tools

Tools that let students adjust materials on their own (e.g., text levelers, translation tools) rather than requiring teacher intervention every time.

Structured Collaboration Roles

When students collaborate or work as partners, give them defined roles, responsibilities, and modeled target behavior so they can practice working together in a structured way.

🤝 What Every Team Asked For

Time to collaborate. Across ELA, Social Studies, and Math, the same answer came up in Step 4: teams don't need outside training — they need dedicated time (Fridays or Wednesdays) to work together, align on common practices, and share what's working.
