👤 One-on-One Intensive Intervention

⭐ Rating: ★★★★★ 📚 Tier: 3 (Intensive) ⏱️ Time: 20–40 min daily 👥 Group Size: 1:1

What It Is

Daily, individualized instruction delivered by a specialist (reading specialist, math interventionist, or special education teacher) targeting a specific, significant skill deficit — typically dyslexia, dyscalculia, or similar. The intervention is diagnostic, cumulative, and data-driven.

Used at AMS by: Math, Science, PE, and Electives list one-on-one or intensive para support as a Tier 3 approach. ELA uses one-on-one reading intervention classes.

Quality Implementation

✅ What TO Do

  • Evidence-based program tied to the specific skill deficit (phonics, fluency, math facts, etc.)
  • Same time, same place, same specialist — consistency drives results
  • Progress monitored weekly; data drives the next session
  • Coordinated with gen-ed — what happens in intervention shows up in the classroom
  • Clear exit criteria: when does the student graduate out?

❌ Red Flags

  • "Homework help" disguised as intervention — doing today's classwork isn't intervention
  • Different adult every week — intensive work requires relationship
  • Pulling student out during their favorite class, every day — damages engagement
  • No exit plan — student stays in Tier 3 indefinitely even when progress has plateaued

Typical Tier 3 Skill Areas

AreaExample Program / Approach
Decoding / phonicsWilson, Orton-Gillingham, SPIRE
Reading fluencyRead Naturally, Repeated Reading
Reading comprehensionStrategic Instruction Model, Reciprocal Teaching
Math facts / computationNumber Worlds, Math Recovery
WritingSelf-Regulated Strategy Development (SRSD)

Scheduling Considerations

  • Pull-out during a non-tested subject when possible
  • Rotate days if pulling from a rotating schedule to avoid missing the same content repeatedly
  • Coordinate with electives and PE so the student doesn't miss the only movement break in their day
  • Communicate with the family about the schedule and the why

Monitoring Success

  • Weekly progress monitoring shows upward trend on the targeted skill
  • Skills transfer to the gen-ed classroom — student uses them unprompted
  • After 6 weeks, if no progress: adjust the program, increase intensity, or evaluate for sped services
  • Exit criteria met: student is reading/computing at grade-level benchmark for 3 consecutive measures
