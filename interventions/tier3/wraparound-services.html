Wraparound Services

Rating: ★★★★★ Tier: 3 (Intensive) Time: Long-term coordination Group Size: Team (school + outside agencies + family)

Treat the specifics — procedures, timelines, role names — as suggestions to check, not as AMS policy.

Verify against the What Works Clearinghouse, the IRIS Center, or Evidence for ESSA — and against AMS’s own procedures — before relying on it with a student.

What It Is

Coordinated support that connects school-based services with outside agencies — mental health providers, medical specialists, social services, community-based organizations, and the family — around a single student. The school doesn't try to do everything; instead, it acts as the coordination hub.

Used at AMS by: Counseling leads wraparound coordination — referrals to school-based or outside therapy, referrals to 504/IEP evaluation, referrals to outside agencies, and collaboration with mental health providers.

When to Consider Wraparound

  • Needs exceed what school-based services alone can address
  • Multiple agencies are already involved but not communicating
  • Family is navigating medical, mental health, housing, or legal systems simultaneously
  • Progress is stalled despite well-implemented Tier 2 and Tier 3 supports
  • Safety concerns require specialized outside expertise

The School's Role

What TO Do

  • Designate a single point of contact (typically the counselor)
  • Obtain written consent from the family before sharing information with outside providers
  • Share relevant school data (attendance, grades, behavior) with consent
  • Schedule joint meetings so the family doesn't have to carry messages between adults
  • Respect the family's leadership — they are the center of the team, not the periphery

Red Flags

  • Making referrals without the family's knowledge or consent
  • Assuming outside providers know the school context — they don't unless you tell them
  • Communicating about a family without including them
  • Overwhelming the family with appointments, forms, and jargon

Coordination Essentials

  • Release of information (ROI): Required before school and outside providers can share info. Counselor handles this.
  • Single shared plan: Everyone on the team works from the same document, not separate plans.
  • Regular check-ins: Monthly or more — coordination fails without ongoing communication.
  • Family-led goals: What does the family want for their child? That's the starting point.

Monitoring Success

  • Family reports feeling supported, not overwhelmed
  • Providers communicate with each other, not just separately with the family
  • Student shows progress across settings — not just at school or just at therapy
  • Crisis frequency decreases; proactive supports are in place
