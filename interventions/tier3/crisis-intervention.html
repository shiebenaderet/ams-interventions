🚨 Crisis Intervention

⭐ Rating: ★★★★★ 📚 Tier: 3 (Intensive) ⏱️ Time: As needed 👥 Group Size: Individual + team

What It Is

Coordinated response to immediate safety concerns — including self-harm, aggression toward others, severe emotional dysregulation, or suicidal ideation. Crisis intervention is always a team response, and at AMS it's led by counseling, administration, and the school psychologist.

Crisis intervention is not a classroom teacher's job alone. If you believe a student is in crisis, your first action is to get the counselor, administrator, or school psychologist involved immediately. Do not try to handle it alone.
Used at AMS by: Counseling leads crisis response and intervention, PREPaRE Team collaboration, threat assessments (with admin and school psych), and suicide assessments & safety plans. Health/PE maintains safety plans for specific students.

When to Call for Help Immediately

  • Student expresses intent to harm self or others
  • Student has a weapon or object being used as a weapon
  • Student is engaged in self-harming behavior
  • Student's emotional state is escalating rapidly and de-escalation strategies aren't working
  • A peer reports any of the above about another student

Call the main office. Get the counselor, administrator, and school psychologist. Do not leave the student alone.

What Classroom Teachers Can Do

During a crisis

  • Stay calm. Your regulation helps the student regulate.
  • Call the office — don't leave the student alone to find help
  • Clear the room of other students if safety requires it
  • Listen. Acknowledge. Don't problem-solve, don't minimize, don't argue.
  • Document what you observed as soon as the immediate crisis passes

After a crisis

  • Debrief with the counselor and psychologist
  • Review any safety plan that is in place; know your role in it
  • Be aware of the student's return plan — smooth re-entry matters
  • Take care of yourself. Crisis response is hard on adults too.

Safety Plans

For students with known crisis history, the counseling team may develop a written safety plan. Classroom teachers typically have a role in it — know that role in advance.

  • Warning signs to watch for
  • Calming strategies the student has agreed to try
  • Pre-arranged safe spaces and adults
  • Specific steps for teachers when signs appear
  • Emergency contacts

Suicide Assessment

If a student expresses suicidal thoughts:

  • Take it seriously. Every time.
  • Don't promise confidentiality — you are a mandated reporter.
  • Get the counselor or psychologist involved immediately.
  • Do not leave the student alone.
  • Document what was said in your own words.

The AMS counseling team uses evidence-based suicide assessment protocols and safety planning.

Remember

You are not alone. Crisis intervention is designed to be a team response. No single teacher is expected to handle a mental health crisis on their own.

Ask for help immediately. The counseling team, admin, and psychologist are trained for this. Get them involved.

Take care of yourself. After a crisis, debrief. Acknowledge the impact. Use EAP or peer support as needed.
