🎨 Choice in Demonstration of Learning

⭐ Rating: ★★★★☆ 📚 Tier: 1 (Universal) ⏱️ Time: Varies by project 👥 Group Size: Individual or groups

What It Is

Students can show understanding through different formats (written, oral, visual, performance) while still meeting the same learning target.

What Quality Implementation Looks Like

✅ In the Classroom (What TO Do):

  • Learning target stays the same (non-negotiable)
  • 2-4 format options provided
  • Clear rubric shows expectations for each format
  • Instruction includes models for each format
  • Choice builds student agency without lowering rigor
  • Students learn to match format to their strengths

❌ Red Flags (What NOT to Do):

  • "Do whatever you want" with no structure
  • Learning target changes based on format (different rigor)
  • Options include only one accessible format (others too hard)
  • No models or instruction for alternative formats
  • Students always pick easiest option
  • Teacher can't assess learning because format obscures understanding

Example 1: Show Understanding of a Historical Event

Learning Target: Explain the causes and effects of the Boston Tea Party
Format Option What Student Creates Rubric Requirements (Same rigor across all)
Written Essay 3-paragraph essay • Claim about causes
• 2 pieces of evidence
• Reasoning connects to effects
• Formal writing conventions
Infographic Visual one-pager with text and images • Title states main idea
• 2+ causes labeled with explanation
• 2+ effects labeled with explanation
• Visual elements support content (not decorative)
• Sources cited
Oral Presentation 3-5 minute speech with notes or slides • Organized: intro, causes, effects, conclusion
• 2+ causes explained clearly
• 2+ effects explained clearly
• Speaks clearly, makes eye contact
Podcast Script 3-5 minute podcast script (can record or submit script) • Hook/intro sets context
• 2+ causes explained conversationally
• 2+ effects explained
• Engaging tone, but historically accurate
All options assessed on: Understanding of causes, understanding of effects, use of evidence, clear explanations

Example 2: Demonstrate Math Understanding

Learning Target: Solve and explain multi-step equations
Format Option What Student Creates Common Requirements
Traditional Problem Set Solve 8 problems, show work • Correct solutions
• All work shown
• 3 problems include written explanation of steps
Teach-Back Video Record yourself solving 4 problems while explaining your thinking • Correct solutions
• Explanation of each step
• At least 2 mistakes caught and corrected
• Clear enough for classmate to learn from
Error Analysis Find errors in 4 solved problems, explain the mistakes, solve correctly • Identify all errors
• Explain why each error is wrong
• Provide correct solution
• Show work
Create Your Own Write 4 multi-step problems with solutions and explanations • Problems vary in difficulty
• Correct solutions provided
• Step-by-step explanations
• Problems follow patterns we studied
All options assessed on: Accuracy, showing mathematical thinking, explaining steps

Implementation Steps

Step 1: Design the Assignment with Choice

  • Start with the Learning Target (non-negotiable)
    What must students know/be able to do?
  • Brainstorm Format Options
    Written, oral, visual, kinesthetic, digital
    Choose 3-4 options that all allow students to meet the target
  • Create Common Rubric
    Content criteria (same for all formats)
    Format-specific criteria (unique to each)
Example Rubric: Criteria | All Formats | Format-Specific ---------|-------------|---------------- Content | • Shows clear understanding of [concept] | Essay: Writing conventions, paragraph structure | • Includes 2+ pieces of evidence | Visual: Organization, labels, design supports content | • Explains reasoning | Oral: Eye contact, volume, pace

Step 2: Teach Students About Choice

Mini-Lesson on Making Strategic Choices (10 min): Teacher: "When I give you choice, how do you decide? Some students always pick the same thing. That's okay, but I want you to think strategically: • Which format helps YOU show what you know best? • Which format challenges you to grow? • Which format matches this content? Let me show you..." [Model thinking through choice for a sample assignment]

Step 3: Provide Models

For each format option:

  • Show example from past student (with permission) OR
  • Create your own example OR
  • Show example from another source

When showing models:

  • Point out what makes it successful
  • Highlight how it meets the learning target
  • Show different quality levels if possible (to calibrate expectations)

Step 4: Build in Checkpoints

Don't wait until final product to see student work:

Checkpoint All Students Purpose
Proposal (Day 1) "I'm choosing [format] because..." Ensure strategic choice
Draft/Outline (Day 3) Rough draft or plan Catch misunderstandings early
Peer Review (Day 5) Partner feedback using rubric Quality check before submission
Final (Day 7) Submit final product Assessment

Step 5: Reflect on Choice

After assignment, students reflect:

  • "Which format did you choose and why?"
  • "Did this format help you show your learning? Why or why not?"
  • "Next time, would you choose the same or try something different?"

This builds metacognition about their learning preferences.

Scaffolding Choice

Level 1: Teacher-Directed

(Start here)

  • Teacher chooses format for all students
  • All students practice same format with support

Level 2: Structured Choice

(What we described above)

  • Teacher provides 2-4 format options
  • All options meet same learning target
  • Clear rubric for each

Level 3: Open Choice

(Advanced)

  • "Show your understanding in any format you choose"
  • Student proposes format
  • Teacher approves if it allows assessment of learning target
Most students need Level 2. Move to Level 3 only when students demonstrate strategic choice-making.

Common Pitfalls & Solutions

❌ Pitfall: Letting students always pick "easy" option

✅ Solution: Require students to try each format at least once per quarter

❌ Pitfall: Options are not equally rigorous

✅ Solution: Use common content rubric; adjust only format-specific criteria

❌ Pitfall: Too many options (choice paralysis)

✅ Solution: Limit to 3-4 clear options

❌ Pitfall: Students don't know what formats "look like"

✅ Solution: Always provide models

❌ Pitfall: Choice becomes more important than learning

✅ Solution: Always center the learning target, not the product
