💬 Turn and Talk / Think-Pair-Share

⭐ Rating: ★★★★☆ 📚 Tier: 1 (Universal) ⏱️ Time: 30 seconds – 2 minutes 👥 Group Size: Partners, whole class

What It Is

A structured partner-discussion protocol that gives every student time to process, articulate, and rehearse their thinking before (or instead of) a whole-class share. Think (silent processing), Pair (share with partner), Share (selected students share with class).

Used at AMS by: Math, Science — as a regular Tier 1 practice for engagement and processing.

Quality Implementation

✅ What TO Do

  • Give a specific prompt, not a vague "talk about this"
  • Name the roles: who speaks first, who listens first
  • Use a visible signal to start and stop (timer, bell, raised hand)
  • Circulate and listen — the teacher is gathering information, not refereeing
  • Call on students whose thinking you heard during the pair, not just volunteers

❌ Red Flags

  • "Talk to your neighbor" with no prompt and no time limit — becomes off-task chatter
  • Only calling on hands after the pair — re-privileges the students who would have spoken anyway
  • Same partners every day — some pairs go stale or avoid each other
  • Too short (under 20 seconds) — students don't have time to formulate a thought

Implementation Approaches

ApproachWhen to Use
Classic Think-Pair-Share
30s silent think → 60s partner share → 2–3 students share out		 Processing a new concept; making meaning of a text
Pair-Share-Switch
Share with one partner, then rotate to a new partner and share again		 Rehearsing an explanation; building fluency with academic language
Stronger-Clearer Each Time
Each round, borrow a phrase or idea from the previous partner		 Writing practice; ELL students building academic discourse
Quick-Check Turn and Talk
"Tell your partner one thing you understand and one thing you're unsure about"		 Formative assessment during a lesson

Step-by-Step

Setting Up Partners

  • Assign partners at the start of a unit — don't force students to choose live
  • Use elbow partners, A/B seating, or clock partners for variety
  • Give each partner a label ("A" and "B") so directions are precise

Running the Protocol

  1. Pose the prompt. Write it on the board so students can refer to it.
  2. Silent think time. 20–60 seconds. Protect this — no hands, no talking.
  3. Pair. "Partner A starts. Partner B, your job is to listen and be ready to add on. 60 seconds."
  4. Switch roles if time allows.
  5. Share. Call on 2–3 specific students — ideally ones you heard good thinking from.

Common Pitfalls & Solutions

One partner dominates

Fix: Assign roles explicitly — "A speaks for 30 seconds, then B speaks for 30 seconds." Use a timer.

Students go off-task

Fix: Shorter time + specific prompt. If talk isn't on-topic in 45 seconds, the prompt is probably too vague.

Same volunteers every share-out

Fix: Cold-call students whose partner-talk you overheard. "Marcus, I heard you say something interesting about…"

Adaptations

For ELL Students

  • Provide sentence frames: "I think ____ because ____."
  • Allow a silent think-write before the pair so students can rehearse
  • Pair strategically with a supportive, patient partner

For Students with IEPs

  • Extend think time
  • Pre-teach the prompt privately so they can prepare
  • Allow written response as an alternative to spoken

Monitoring Success

  • 80%+ of students are on-task during pair time (walk the room and count)
  • Share-outs come from a wider set of students than whole-class hands alone
  • Student writing or exit tickets show evidence of ideas heard during pair talk
