📋 Behavior Check-Ins (Check-In/Check-Out)

⭐ Rating: ★★★★☆ 📚 Tier: 2 (Targeted) ⏱️ Time: 2–5 min start & end of day 👥 Group Size: Individual

What It Is

A structured daily routine where a student meets briefly with a consistent adult at the start of the day to set a behavior goal, carries a point sheet through the day for teacher feedback, and meets again at the end of the day to review. Often called Check-In/Check-Out (CICO), it is one of the most evidence-based Tier 2 behavior supports.

Used at AMS by: ELA and Social Studies explicitly list behavior check-ins as a Tier 2 must-have. Health/PE uses a "behavior tracking chart." Science identified CICO as a strategy to explore.

Quality Implementation

✅ What TO Do

  • Same adult, same time, every day — consistency is the whole point
  • 3 or fewer goals on the point sheet, stated positively ("use a quiet voice" not "don't yell")
  • Every teacher gives feedback during class transitions — quick, specific, non-shaming
  • End-of-day meeting focuses on the data and the student's reflection, not the adult's judgment
  • Build in a small reinforcer for meeting the goal (not punishment for missing it)

❌ Red Flags

  • Point sheet becomes a referral tool — "you lost a point" used as a threat
  • Different adult every morning — kills the relationship-building that makes CICO work
  • Goals too vague ("be good") or too many (8 things to track per period)
  • Teachers who refuse to sign or give feedback — one non-participant undermines the whole system

The CICO Cycle

Morning check-in (2 min, same adult): Review yesterday's progress. Set today's goals (3 max). Encouragement. Throughout the day: At the end of each period, teacher rates the goals (0 / 1 / 2) and adds a brief note. End of day (3–5 min, same adult): Review point sheet. Student reflects: "What went well? What was hard?" If goal met, small reinforcer. Weekly: Share data with family (copy home Friday). Every 2 weeks, team reviews: is it working?

Common Pitfalls

Student "games" the point sheet

Fix: The sheet is a conversation tool, not a contract. Focus end-of-day on reflection, not just the number.

Teachers forget to sign

Fix: Point sheet stays visible on the student's desk; student hands it to the teacher at dismissal. Student ownership = higher compliance.

Student refuses to participate

Fix: Find the right mentor — CICO lives or dies on relationship. Let the student have a say in who their check-in adult is.

When to Escalate

If after 6–8 weeks of consistent implementation the behavior isn't improving:
  • Review fidelity — is the intervention actually being done as designed?
  • Revisit the goals — are they specific and achievable?
  • Consult with the counseling team; consider a Behavior Intervention Plan at Tier 3

Monitoring Success

  • Student meets daily goal 80%+ of the time for 3 consecutive weeks
  • Referrals or incidents decrease
  • Student can articulate their goals and what helps them meet them
  • Exit plan: fade to every-other-day, then weekly, then off
