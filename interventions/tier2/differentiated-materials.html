📚 Differentiated Materials

⭐ Rating: ★★★★☆ 📚 Tier: 2 (Targeted) ⏱️ Time: Planning-level 👥 Group Size: Individual or small group

What It Is

Providing the same content at different complexity levels (reading difficulty, problem complexity, scaffolding amount) so students can access grade-level concepts even when they're not yet reading or computing at grade level.

Used at AMS by: All core subjects. Science uses Amplify's read-aloud and Spanish versions; Social Studies allows students to select Lexile levels; ELA uses tiered texts in reading intervention classes.

Quality Implementation

✅ What TO Do

  • Same learning target, different entry point — complexity varies, rigor stays
  • Use tools that let students self-select level (Magic School AI text leveler, Newsela, Readworks)
  • Teach students how to pick a level that's challenging but accessible
  • Rotate which students use which materials — avoid fixed "low/middle/high" groups

❌ Red Flags

  • Lower-level materials that cover less content — that's modification, not differentiation
  • Same students always get the "easy" version — becomes a tracked ceiling
  • Visually obvious that materials are different — students know who got "the easy one"
  • No plan to fade — differentiation becomes a permanent scaffold

Approaches

TypeHow
Reading levelMagic School AI text leveler, Newsela, CommonLit — same article, 3+ Lexile levels.
Scaffolded notesFull notes, skeletal notes, blank outline — student chooses what they need.
Problem complexitySame math concept, varied numbers / multi-step vs. single-step.
Translated materialsKey documents in Spanish or other home languages. Immersive Reader on Canvas reads aloud and translates.

Implementation

  1. Identify the learning target. What must every student be able to do at the end? That's fixed.
  2. Identify the barrier. Is it decoding? Vocabulary? Prior knowledge? Procedural fluency? The answer drives which differentiation tool to use.
  3. Provide options, not assignments. "Here are three versions — pick the one that challenges you without overwhelming you." Teach students to self-select.
  4. Check comprehension at the same ceiling. Assessment items should be the same or equivalent across levels.

Monitoring Success

  • Students reach the same learning target regardless of entry point
  • Students gradually move up levels over the year (not static)
  • Self-selection feels low-stakes — students can pick up or down without shame
