🧩 Chunking

⭐ Rating: ★★★★☆ 📚 Tier: 2 (Targeted) ⏱️ Time: Planning-level 👥 Group Size: Individual or small group

What It Is

Breaking a complex task, text, or assignment into smaller, manageable steps so that students can complete each piece before moving to the next. Chunking reduces cognitive load and prevents the "I don't know where to start" shutdown.

Used at AMS by: ELA, Math, Science, Social Studies — consistently across all core subjects.

Quality Implementation

✅ What TO Do

  • Break the task at natural decision points, not arbitrary word counts
  • Make each chunk a self-contained mini-goal with a clear "done" signal
  • Build in check-ins after each chunk (even if just a visual review)
  • Show the student the whole task first, then the chunks — they need the map

❌ Red Flags

  • Chunking = giving less work. It's the same rigor, delivered in pieces.
  • Chunks so small they feel condescending or infantilizing
  • No check-ins between chunks — student finishes wrong and has to redo all of it
  • Only chunking for Tier 2 students while the rest of the class gets the whole task at once (stigmatizing)

Approaches

Type of ChunkingExample
Text chunkingBreak a 4-page reading into 4 single-page chunks, each with a comprehension question.
Assignment chunkingA 5-paragraph essay becomes: thesis → outline → intro → body → conclusion, each due separately.
Problem-set chunkingAssign 3 problems, check for accuracy, then release the next 3.
Visual chunkingUse boxes, highlighting, or fold-and-cover to isolate one section of a worksheet at a time.

Step-by-Step

  1. Identify the "choke point." Where do students typically give up or get lost? That's where the chunk boundary goes.
  2. Number the chunks visibly on the page: "Part 1 of 4."
  3. Give a done signal after each chunk — highlight, checkbox, or teacher initial.
  4. Rebuild the whole at the end — have students review what they've completed as one piece.

Common Pitfalls

Student finishes each chunk but can't see the whole

Fix: At the end, do a "zoom out" — review all chunks together as one coherent task.

Chunking becomes a permanent crutch

Fix: Fade the scaffold. Start with 4 chunks, then 2, then let the student chunk it themselves.

Other students notice and ask for it

Fix: Offer chunked versions as an option for everyone. Good Tier 1 practice.

Monitoring Success

  • Task completion rate increases
  • Student begins chunking independently (asks for "just the first part")
  • Quality of work per chunk is comparable to peers — chunking doesn't lower the ceiling
