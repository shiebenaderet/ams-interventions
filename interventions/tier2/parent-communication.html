Individualized Parent/Guardian Communication

Rating: ★★★★☆ Tier: 2 (Targeted) Time: 5–15 min per contact Group Size: Individual

Treat the specifics — procedures, timelines, role names — as suggestions to check, not as AMS policy.

Verify against the What Works Clearinghouse, the IRIS Center, or Evidence for ESSA — and against AMS’s own procedures — before relying on it with a student.

What It Is

Regular, targeted communication with a specific student's family — beyond whole-class communication like ParentSquare blasts. The goal is partnership: shared understanding of the student, coordinated support at home and school, and catching issues early.

Used at AMS by: Social Studies and Electives specifically call out individualized family communication as a Tier 2 must-have. ELA, Math, and PE also use it. Electives notes: contact family at home for additional support/guidance.

Quality Implementation

What TO Do

  • Lead with a positive — even if the reason you're calling is a concern
  • Be specific: "Marcus turned in his essay on Tuesday and did a great job with the thesis" — not "he had a good week"
  • Ask, don't just tell: "What does homework time look like at home? What's working?"
  • Use the family's preferred language (ParentSquare translates; interpreters available through the FRA)
  • Document the contact — date, topic, agreed next steps
  • Follow up when you said you would

Red Flags

  • Only calling home when there's a problem — builds a "the teacher only calls when it's bad" pattern
  • Communicating in English when the family's primary language is something else
  • Asking the student to translate for their parent — inappropriate and ineffective
  • Setting up an expectation you can't sustain (weekly calls for one family and nothing for others)

Approaches

MethodWhen
Positive call homeFirst two weeks of a Tier 2 plan — builds goodwill before any concerns arise
Weekly email or ParentSquare messageOngoing progress updates; translates automatically
Scheduled meetingComplex situations; when multiple teachers need to be in the room
Home visit (with FRA)Attendance or engagement concerns; cultural or language barriers to school meetings

Step-by-Step

  1. First contact is positive. Introduce yourself, share one specific thing you appreciate about the student.
  2. Set a rhythm. "I'll check in every other Friday. What's the best way to reach you?"
  3. Share data, not vibes. "He completed 4 of 5 assignments last week" is usable. "He's been doing better" is not.
  4. Ask for partnership. "What would help at home? What have you tried that works?"
  5. Loop in the counselor if anything comes up beyond classroom concerns.

Monitoring Success

  • Family responds to contacts and engages in the conversation
  • Student's behavior or work shows improvement aligned with the home-school plan
  • Family reaches out proactively when something changes

Resources

  • ParentSquare — automatic translation, message archive
  • Family Resource Advocate (FRA) — home visits, interpreter coordination
  • Counseling team — point of contact when communication goes beyond academics
