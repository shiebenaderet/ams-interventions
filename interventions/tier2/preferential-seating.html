Preferential Seating

Rating: ★★★☆☆ Tier: 2 (Targeted) Time: One-time setup Group Size: Individual

Verify against the What Works Clearinghouse, the IRIS Center, or Evidence for ESSA — and against AMS’s own procedures — before relying on it with a student.

What It Is

Strategically placing a student based on a specific need — attention, behavior, engagement, hearing/vision, or proximity to a supportive peer. Often an IEP or 504 accommodation, but also a useful Tier 2 tool when used intentionally.

Used at AMS by: Science, Social Studies, PE — commonly deployed as a light-touch Tier 2 support. Health/PE also calls it "moving seat."

Quality Implementation

What TO Do

  • Identify the reason before picking a seat — "front row" isn't automatically right
  • Match the seat to the need: near the teacher for attention, away from a window for focus, near a positive peer for modeling
  • Talk to the student privately about why and ask their input
  • Revisit every few weeks — what worked in September may not in March

Red Flags

  • Seating charts that obviously single out students ("the kid by the teacher's desk")
  • Using seating as a punishment rather than a support
  • Never changing the arrangement — the student stops noticing and the effect wears off
  • Isolating a student in a corner "to keep them from bothering others" — that's exclusion, not intervention

Matching Need to Seat

Student NeedSeating Strategy
Attention / distractibilityNear teacher, away from high-traffic areas and windows
Hearing or visionFront, center, with clear line of sight to board
AnxietyNear an exit or wall; not in the middle of the room
Needs peer modelingNext to a strong, patient peer (ask the peer first)
Needs movementSeat near the back or an aisle where standing isn't disruptive

Monitoring Success

  • Observable reduction in the specific behavior the seat was chosen to address
  • Student reports feeling more able to focus/participate
  • If no change after 2–3 weeks, the seat probably isn't the issue — look at other factors
